    Five billion-dollar markets. We're already paid in two.

    We're not betting on which one wins. We're building the platform that can move between them.

    GA flight training spends $1.5B to $2B a year, and GA insurance spends $2.9B to $3.2B a year. We already have paying customers in both.

    Three more sit next to us: drone training at $0.5B, US flight simulation at $1.7B to $2.2B, and autonomous aircraft, the newest and largest, already at $10B.

    The industry underneath isn't working: schools rate 3.4 out of 5, only half who start ever finish, and two thirds of examiners say new pilots are worse prepared than five years ago.

    We don't know which market matters most in five years. We know we're already inside two of them, earning trust.

    They give the what. We deliver the how.

    The ACS, practical test standards and the FAA's own currency requirements all say what a pilot must be able to do. None of them says how to go about it.

    Checkrides are short episodes in a pilot's career, not a destination. We built for the entire journey. A pilot's learning path runs from that initial spark, through certification, into decades of staying proficient and capable of safe flight.

    We surround that path with a flight training network of instructors, educators and specialists who step in wherever it calls for them, and with a discipline for how every answer is built: the Learn-Do-Fly Training Standard, written by master instructor Rich Stowell over a forty-year period, the regulations and doctrine behind it, and the judgment of named subject matter experts, all broken down and cited rather than guessed at. That is what provides the guardrails that keep an answer honest, and the truth a pilot can build a lifetime of flying on.

    That discipline runs through three parts of the technology.

    People are already paying, each starting somewhere different.

    We don't know yet how big our share of this market will be. We know the system is broken, and we're already inside it.

    AeroElite, a Part 141 and Part 61 flight school with eight locations in the greater Washington, DC area, started with our resource management tool and is moving its operations onto our Flight Ops platform. The University of Maine at Augusta bought seats on Loops for its entire incoming class, and every student is now activated. Both are now extending into the parts they didn't start with.

    Avemco, the only direct writer of general aviation aircraft insurance in the US, already discounts our courses by up to 10 percent, and we are working with them to extend that to our whole training method.
